A quick guide for graduate students

AI in research:
use the right level of help

AI can accelerate searching, coding, debugging, and organization. Researchers must still decide the question, method, evidential limits, and final claims.

One bottom line: hypotheses, analysis plans, and exclusion rules can be clarified and stress-tested with AI before data collection. Do not let AI rewrite post hoc decisions as if they had been predicted in advance.

Choose the level of help

  1. 01

    Organize and discuss

    Summarize readings, compare concepts, ask Socratic questions, and polish drafts. Verify every citation against the original source.

  2. 02

    Generate and check

    Draft scripts, data dictionaries, checklists, and plots; ask AI to challenge your reasoning. Test generated code and inspect outputs.

  3. 03

    Run multi-step technical work

    Use AI for iterative preprocessing, statistical pipelines, and debugging. Validate every parameter, data mapping, and result.

Eight research stages

  1. Positioning and question formation: map literature and candidate gaps; you judge whether the gap is real and important.
  2. Design and planning: compare tasks and validity threats; you decide operationalization, effect-size assumptions, and exclusion rules.
  3. Pilot and method confirmation: write experimental code and QC checks; verify timing, triggers, equipment, and thresholds in practice.
  4. Registration and data collection: format preregistration and checklists; do not share identifiable personal data with AI.
  5. Data preparation and QC: assist with BIDS, scripts, logs, and READMEs; do not change rules after seeing the data.
  6. Analysis and evaluation: write models and robustness analyses; distinguish confirmatory from exploratory work and test code with known answers.
  7. Interpretation and next study: list alternatives and limits; reserve revised hypotheses for the next round.
  8. Reporting and communication: polish prose and organize reviews; verify methods, numbers, citations, disclosures, and confidentiality.

Plans and workload

Free is suitable for occasional queries and short edits. Pro is generally suited to ordinary graduate-student work with Research, Projects, or Claude Code. Max mainly provides more usage for long debugging sessions. Team and Enterprise make sense only when shared workspace, administration, or compliance needs justify them.

Plans, models, prices, and limits change. This summary follows the source document’s check on 2026-09-11; verify current details on the official Claude pricing page. No model or plan makes generated citations, code, numbers, or methods automatically trustworthy.

Before submission: five questions

  1. Can I explain each major decision?
  2. Were hypotheses, analyses, and exclusions set before seeing the data?
  3. Are exploratory analyses clearly marked?
  4. Have I checked every citation, number, figure, and script?
  5. Have I followed privacy, ethics, journal, and peer-review rules?
