Literature AI services on one page: Elicit, Scite Assistant, Consensus

Compiled 11 September 2026 from each vendor's site and help center plus university-library evaluations. All three change quickly; the vendor sites are the final word. Only Scite's pricing was read from the official page on the writing date; Elicit's and Consensus's pricing pages would not load and third-party figures conflict, so no amounts are given.

Elicitelicit.com Scite Assistantscite.ai/assistant Consensusconsensus.app
Built for The review workflow: semantic search → screen → extract custom fields from each paper into a table → report. A Research Agent (Aug 2026) chains the steps. Question answering on a citation-context platform. Smart Citations classify each citing sentence as supporting, contrasting, or mentioning, with its section. Search engine plus answer layer. The Consensus Meter sorts the top 20 papers on a yes/no question into Yes / No / Possibly / Mixed and shows proportions.
Data and full text 138M papers (Semantic Scholar, PubMed, OpenAlex) plus 545K clinical trials. Full text for open access; subscription journals need the browser extension plus an institutional subscription, otherwise abstract only. 317M+ indexed works, about 41M with full text (including paywalled content from 35+ publisher partners). Works without full text have metadata only, so no citation sentences. 220M+ papers (Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, own crawl, 7 partner publishers), updated weekly; Medical Mode: about 8M papers plus 50K clinical guidelines. Full text only from partners or open access.
Main functions Custom-column extraction (including uploaded PDFs); systematic-review workflow (PRISMA 2020, auditable); Reports with control over included papers; Alerts; Library; collaboration; API and MCP; CSV/BIB export. Cited synthesis with the search strategy shown; settings for model, paper count, years, type, journal, or a Collection/DOI list; Table Mode with custom columns and CSV export; Fact checks listing kept and rejected sources; PDF upload; Reference Check for retractions and editorial notices; Collection alerts; Zotero plugin; MCP. Pro synthesis; Meter plus Snapshot (per-position mean year, methods quality, SJR, citations); Study Snapshot; filters for design, sample size, Q1–Q4; Chat with full text (including uploads); Deep Search; Research Agent (citation tracing, author analysis, comparison tables); Medical Mode; export to Zotero and others.
Caveats Abstract-only without full text, so methods details are often missing. Accuracy figures (994 Cochrane reviews: 95% recall, 96% extraction) are vendor self-evaluations, not independently replicated. A thousand papers is not an exhaustive search; formal reviews still need traditional databases and dual screening. Check agent-made figures against the source. Credits go fast. Uploading paywalled papers raises licence questions. Fact checks do not guarantee relevance: an HKU Libraries test (June 2026) saw an unrelated paper marked "verified". Preprints and zero-citation papers are included by default; exclude them or rank by citations. Citation classes are model judgements ("contrasting" may be a methods aside), so read the sentence. Papers without full text have no contrasting-citation record, so "no contradiction" is not confirmation. Thin non-English coverage. No permanent free tier. The Meter reflects only 5–20 papers and shifts when the question is rephrased (the vendor's first-listed limitation). Classification ignores conditions in the question (vendor example: a paediatric study counted as "Yes" to an adult-safety question). Yes/no turns "it depends" into a percentage; a vote count is not a pooled effect size. Ranking favours recent, highly cited papers, so pivotal older work can drop out. Preprint handling is undocumented. Syntheses occasionally cite papers for things they do not say.
Best fit Screening and extraction for systematic or scoping reviews; meta-analysis coding sheets (define fields like a coding manual); first-draft skeletons for grant backgrounds or reviews; monitoring new papers; turning a batch of PDFs into a table.
Not for: one "is there evidence?" question; how a paper was later cited.		 Checking whether a finding was later supported or contradicted before citing it; a retraction audit of the reference list before submission; counter-evidence for the Discussion, read in the original sentences; tracking the citation character of your own papers; teaching citation count versus citation character.
Not for: non-English or grey literature; free long-term use; quick syntheses where context does not matter.		 A minutes-long read on which way the evidence leans; the first hour in an unfamiliar field; clinical questions in Medical Mode; snowballing from a classic paper; a first comparison table before moving to Elicit; quick checks on "everyone knows" claims while reviewing or supervising.
Not for: formal reviews needing reproducible search records; debates that cannot be phrased yes/no.
Pricing Free credits plus paid plans; monthly usage pool since June 2026; amounts not verified. Paid after a 7-day trial: Basic US$20/month, Pro US$50/month (billed yearly; verified Sept 2026). The free Connect plan gives 25 MCP credits a month, no Assistant. Free credits plus paid plans; Deep Search and Research Agent are paid modes with monthly caps; amounts not verified.

Shared by all three: outputs are not citable sources — go back to the paper and confirm it says that, under matching conditions; "with citations" is not "correctly cited"; all rest on Semantic Scholar/OpenAlex plus publisher deals, so Chinese-language journals, book chapters, and grey literature are weakly covered; if a tool appears in a Methods section, record the query, date, settings, and version (the same question a week later can return different results); read the privacy terms before uploading unpublished work (Consensus states it does not train on user data; the other two were not checked); disclose AI use as the journal requires.
